## Countercase
The 43% FCF yield is a TTM figure from a business whose key product patents (Nucynta, amortized through 2028-09-22) end in under two years; generics would remove a large share of cash flow while the 1.1B debt stays, so equity could be worth far less than TTM FCF multiples imply. Base revenue excluding Azstarys fell about 9% in Q3 and SG&A rose 48%. Operating income halved for the 9M. GAAP profit relies on a tax benefit and deferred taxes (cash taxes unread). Opioid exposure: new Pennsylvania payor suit (2026-07-27, removed to federal court 2026-10-19), four state AG investigations, Belbuca paragraph IV challenges (Ascent June 2026, patent to 2032), regulatory and reputational risk to opioid sellers, sole-source suppliers. The ASR and 50M buyback while levered, and working-capital timing of rebates (liability up 109M since year end) could reverse. Product revenue by brand for Xtampza and Nucynta, Q4 guidance, peers (e.g. Pacira, Supernus, Jazz) and the 10-K are unread.

## What would change the view
The cheap-on-cash case breaks if Nucynta revenue decline starts before 2028, if litigation produces a settlement of material size, or if debt paydown stalls. It strengthens if Azstarys and Jornay growth offsets Nucynta runoff and the term loan is retired faster than guided.
