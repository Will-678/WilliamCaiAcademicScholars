## Countercase
A single product with a patent cliff in 2029 to 2030 means the low earnings multiple partly reflects limited remaining duration: gross margin is falling (74.7% versus 79.2%), operating costs are rising ahead of revenue (R&D +34% in nine months), and Q3 operating income fell 17% on revenue growth of 12%. Revenue growth already slowed from 19% in nine months to 12% in Q3. Three specialty pharmacies make up 100% of gross receivables (CVS Caremark 42%, Accredo 38%, PANTHERx 20%), so a payer or channel change would bite. Nine month interest income of 21.3M flatters pretax profit, and the large cash pile is only valuable if deployed well: management says it will pursue business development, and a large acquisition or licensing deal would change the picture (nine month IPR&D and license payments already total 74M or more). The stock fell 12% between 2026-11-03 (37.02) and 2026-11-05 (32.47) after results and the CEO news; I did not establish why beyond the figures above. BP-205 and Pitolisant GR may not succeed, and competing orexin agonists from larger companies could displace WAKIX. The strong TTM cash flow does not subtract future spending on R&D that is likely to rise. A cheap multiple on a patent-limited single product is not by itself a margin of safety.

## What would invalidate it
Quarterly WAKIX revenue growth falling below about 5%, patient adds below 300 per quarter, loss of generic settlement protections or earlier generic entry, gross margin below 70% on a sustained basis, a large cash-funded acquisition that destroys value, or competitor orexin agonist approval that displaces WAKIX. Supportive: continued 400 patient adds, Pitolisant GR approval in April 2027 extending exclusivity, and disciplined capital deployment.
